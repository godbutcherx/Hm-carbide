Micro tools: why ultrafine grain

The smaller the diameter, the more the microstructure decides edge quality and tool life.

15 November 2026 · HM Carbide Swiss GmbH

On micro drills and micro end mills the cutting edge is often only a fraction of a millimetre long. At this scale the grain size of the carbide matters in a way that hardly shows on large tools. For small diameters, ultrafine grain is therefore usually the first choice.

The edge is only as fine as the grain

Carbide consists of tungsten carbide grains embedded in cobalt. When the tool is ground, the cutting edge forms along these grains. The coarser the grain, the more “grainy” the edge and the more easily individual grains break out. On a ø 20 mm end mill this barely matters. On a ø 1 mm tool it decides whether the edge stays sharp and stable.

Ultrafine grain with a grain size of 0.4 µm therefore allows sharper, more uniform edges and smaller edge radii. It is also harder and more wear-resistant than coarser grades, which extends tool life on small tools with little material at the edge.

Which grade?

For micro tools we recommend our ultrafine grades:

  • HM 12Co: 88% WC, 12% Co, grain size 0.4 µm, hardness 92.5 ± 0.5 HRA, transverse rupture strength >3432 N/mm².
  • HM12F: ultrafine grain, 0.4 µm.

The 12% cobalt content gives the fine structure the toughness it needs. That matters for micro tools, because the small cross-section can hardly absorb bending loads and vibration. Full data is in the grade guide.

Tolerance h6 on small diameters

Our rods are ground to h6 on the diameter. For diameters from 1 to 3 mm, ISO 286 gives a deviation of 0 to −6 µm: a ø 2 mm rod measures between 1.994 and 2.000 mm. For micro tools, which are often clamped to very tight runout tolerances, a closely toleranced shank is the basis for clean runout.

Handling small diameters

Hard ultrafine carbide is less forgiving than tougher grades. With thin rods, care pays off:

  • Store rods individually or in tubes so they do not knock against each other.
  • When grinding, use rigid clamping and a short overhang to avoid vibration.
  • Check the end faces for chipped edges before clamping.
