What is cemented carbide?

Cemented carbide is a composite of very hard tungsten carbide grains (WC) and a metallic binder, usually cobalt (Co). WC and Co powder is pressed and sintered; the cobalt surrounds the carbide grains and holds them together. The result is a material that is considerably harder and more wear-resistant than high-speed steel.

Carbide rods are the blanks from which toolmakers grind drills, end mills, reamers and special tools. How a tool performs later depends heavily on two variables: the grain size of the tungsten carbide and the cobalt content.

Grain size: hardness versus toughness

The finer the carbide grains, the harder and more wear-resistant the carbide, and the sharper the edge that can be ground. Coarser grain makes the material tougher and less sensitive to impact, but less wear-resistant.

  • Ultrafine grain (0.4 µm): hard and wear-resistant, for sharp edges and small diameters.
  • Fine grain (0.6 µm): balanced between hardness and toughness; the most requested class.
  • Medium grain (0.8 µm): tough and fracture-resistant, for tools under high impact and bending loads.

Cobalt content: the binder

Cobalt provides toughness. At the same grain size, more cobalt means higher fracture resistance and less tendency to edge chipping, but slightly lower hardness. Less cobalt makes the carbide harder and more brittle.

Grain size and cobalt content always work together. An example from our range: HM 12Co contains more cobalt than HM 10Co and is still harder, because its 0.4 µm grain is much finer than the 0.8 µm grain of HM 10Co. Here the grain size more than offsets the higher binder content.

Tolerance h6: what it means

Our rods are ground to diameter tolerance h6 according to ISO 286. The “h” means the upper deviation is zero, so the rod is never larger than the nominal size. The “6” stands for tolerance grade IT6 and defines how far below nominal the diameter may be.

Example: a ø 10 mm rod in h6 measures between 9.991 and 10.000 mm. For toolmakers this means the shank fits shrink-fit and hydraulic chucks without rework.

Rods with coolant channels

With deep holes and high cutting speeds, chips and heat have to leave the cutting zone quickly. Rods with internal coolant channels bring coolant directly to the cutting edge. This lowers the temperature, improves chip evacuation and extends tool life.

We supply coolant-channel rods from ø 4 to 25 mm with channel diameters from 0.5 to 2.0 mm. All sizes with channel diameter and channel spacing are listed on the product page.
